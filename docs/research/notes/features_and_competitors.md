# Features users want and competitors in bilingual / dual-subtitle tools for streaming video

Research date: 2026-10-05. About 20 tool calls. Chrome Web Store (CWS) numbers were read directly from the listings on this date unless marked otherwise. Many "comparison" articles come from vendors (Trancy, Lingopie, FluentAI, Funlingo, Linglass), so they are flagged as biased.

## (a) Which features do users most request or praise, and how do learners differ from viewers who just want to understand the show?

### Takeaway
Evidence for what learners value most points to five features: (1) two subtitle lines shown at the same time; (2) hover or click word lookup in a popup dictionary; (3) saving words with Anki or flashcard export; (4) playback controls for learning, such as auto-pause after each line, replaying a line, and hiding a line until hover; (5) subtitle styling and position. The newest selling point that vendors push is LLM translation that uses context, and AI transcription for videos that have no captions. Viewers who only want to understand the show (families, immigrants, people who want their native language) are mostly served by simple Hulu-only extensions that offer bilingual display and styling only. No source I found gives first-hand user statements about what this group wants.

### Cited Findings
**Learner features that are praised or advertised (ranked roughly by how often they appear across sources)**
1. **Two subtitle lines at once.** This is the core value. "Netflix only displays one subtitle track at a time… you need a dedicated extension that intercepts the video player and overlays a second subtitle stream" — [Funlingo blog](https://www.getfunlingo.com/blog/netflix-dual-subtitles) / [Linglass blog](https://linglass.app/blog/netflix-dual-subtitles) (vendor). SubText describes itself as "shows two subtitle lines at once, the original and a translation" — [AMO SubText](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/subtext/).
2. **Hover or click popup dictionary.** In Language Reactor, "When users encounter an unknown word in the subtitles or transcript, they simply hover over it and an L1 definition appears" — [JALT article](https://jalt-publications.org/articles/28605-enhancing-functionality-youtube-and-netflix-language-reactor). Mate: "Click a word and playback pauses with a tooltip showing the translation, pronunciation and synonyms" — [Mate help](https://matetranslate.com/en/help/translate-netflix-subtitles). InterSub offers hover definitions — [AMO InterSub](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/intersub/).
3. **Saving vocabulary and exporting to Anki.** Language Reactor can "export beautiful flashcards to Anki with screenshots and audio clips" — [languavibe review 2026](https://languavibe.com/language-reactor-review/); [LTL school](https://ltl-school.com/language-reactor/). A Trancy comparison concedes that Language Reactor is "better for serious polyglots who want the strongest Anki export workflow" — [Trancy blog (vendor), May 2026](https://www.trancy.org/blog/7-best-language-reactor-alternatives-compared-2026-3609d2252005811db7f7d41131756428). InterSub exports vocabulary to Excel and syncs with AnkiDroid — [AMO InterSub](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/intersub/). Mate requires Pro to save words — [Mate help](https://matetranslate.com/en/help/translate-netflix-subtitles).
4. **Playback controls for learning.** Language Reactor's "auto-pause after each subtitle until you continue" and "move your cursor over subtitles to reveal them—perfect for improving your listening skills" (a hide-until-hover mode) — [LTL school](https://ltl-school.com/language-reactor/); its CWS listing advertises "precise video playback controls" — [CWS Language Reactor](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/language-reactor/hoombieeljmmljlkjmnheibnpciblicm).
5. **Subtitle styling and position** (color, size, opacity, background, position). This is the headline feature of the Hulu-only extensions — [CWS Hulu Dual Subtitles](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/llbekaiifiopdmojkacmkaihdenoffnh). InterSub has opacity, colors, outlines, and RTL optimization — [AMO InterSub](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/intersub/).
6. **AI transcription when captions are missing or poor.** On the LR forum (Dec 2025 to May 2026), broken ASR is a major complaint. Subsmith was recommended because it runs Whisper locally — [LR forum thread](https://forum.languagelearningwithnetflix.com/t/language-reactor-alternative/36445). Immersive Translate's Hulu page says it can "automatically generate accurate subtitles using advanced AI speech recognition" for content without captions — [Immersive Translate Hulu page](https://immersivetranslate.com/en/video/hulu-subtitles-translator/). Trancy's free tier allows "40 AI-transcribed videos per day" — [Trancy blog (vendor)](https://www.trancy.org/blog/free-netflix-dual-subtitle-extension-no-language-reactor-31f9d22520058039b157f18a77ca6534).
7. **AI grammar or context explanations.** InterSub's "ChatGPT-powered AI Language Buddy for translations, grammar explanations, and cultural context" — [AMO InterSub](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/intersub/). Trancy offers word and sentence translation with grammar analysis — [CWS Trancy](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/trancy-ai-translator-dual/mjdbhokoopacimoekfgkcoogikbfgngb).
8. **Downloading or exporting subtitles.** The Hulu Dual Subtitles extension lists "Subtitle download capability" — [CWS Hulu Dual Subtitles](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/llbekaiifiopdmojkacmkaihdenoffnh).
9. **Pronunciation and pitch accent tools (Japanese and Korean).** Migaku is noted for "pitch-accent tools, sentence mining" — [Trancy blog (vendor)](https://www.trancy.org/blog/7-best-language-reactor-alternatives-compared-2026-3609d2252005811db7f7d41131756428). I found no direct user evidence on pinyin or furigana demand in this pass.
10. **Speaking practice and pronunciation scoring.** Trancy promotes this as a differentiator — [CWS Trancy](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/trancy-ai-translator-dual/mjdbhokoopacimoekfgkcoogikbfgngb). This is vendor-driven rather than user-requested.

**Viewers who just want to understand the show**
- The InterSub blog says dual subtitles are "a fantastic tool for multilingual families, making content more accessible and enjoyable without language barriers" — [InterSub blog](https://blog.intersub.cc/transform-your-streaming-experience-using-dual-subtitles-on-netflix-and-beyond/) (vendor framing).
- The Hulu-specific extensions emphasize styling, position, and download rather than learning tools. They target "language learners or anyone who enjoys understanding both the original script and its translation" — [CWS Hulu Dual Subtitles](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/llbekaiifiopdmojkacmkaihdenoffnh).
- These extensions work only in desktop browsers, "not the Netflix mobile or smart TV apps" — [Linglass blog](https://linglass.app/blog/netflix-dual-subtitles). That matters for family living-room viewing.

**Context-aware or whole-episode LLM translation as a differentiator**
- Immersive Translate's Hulu page: "The AI models analyze entire conversation threads rather than translating line by line, which helps maintain narrative coherence and character voice consistency." It offers 20+ engines including ChatGPT, DeepL, DeepSeek, and Gemini — [Immersive Translate Hulu](https://immersivetranslate.com/en/video/hulu-subtitles-translator/). The CWS listing also names Claude — [CWS Immersive Translate](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/immersive-translate-trans/bpoadfkcbjbfhfodiogcnhhhpibjhbnh).
- The small DualSub (a different product from Dualsub Studio) advertises "AI context analysis powered by OpenAI GPT or Google Gemini" with Google, Microsoft, DeepL, and OpenAI providers — [extensionauditor DualSub](https://extensionauditor.com/scan/dualsub-lnkcpcbpjbidpjdjnmjdllpkgpocaikj).
- FluentAI advertises "context-aware translations" — [FluentAI guide (vendor)](https://fluentai.pro/guides/dual-subtitles-chrome-extension). Trancy's premium tier "adds GPT-5-mini access" — [Trancy vs LR (vendor)](https://www.trancy.org/blog/language-reactor-vs-trancy-complete-comparison-guide-3609d225200581e38e12da72010b2448).

### Inferences
- Learner tooling (lookup, Anki, auto-pause) is crowded and dominated by Language Reactor, Trancy, eJOY, and Migaku. Viewers who only want to understand the show are served mainly by tiny, rarely updated Hulu extensions with fewer than 1K users, which focus on styling. That is a gap for a polished Hulu tool built for understanding rather than learning.
- Vendors market "context-aware LLM translation" heavily, but I found no organic user threads that praise it specifically. It is an untested differentiator in user voice, not a proven one.
- Free tiers with explicit daily caps (Trancy: 40 videos/day; InterSub: 15 lookups/month) suggest users accept quotas when LLM costs are involved.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Reddit directly in this pass. There are no first-hand Reddit quotes on "dual subtitles Hulu" or on parents and immigrants using these tools. The evidence for viewers who only want to understand the show is vendor framing.
- I found no evidence on demand for per-show glossaries, offline mode, multiple target languages at once, or choosing which line sits on top.
- I could not read individual CWS review texts because the listing pages did not expose them through the fetch tool.

## (b) Competitor matrix

### Takeaway
Language Reactor (2M CWS users) is still the largest dedicated learning tool, but a 2025 to 2026 forum thread calls it stagnant. Trancy (300K CWS users, 4.7 stars) and Immersive Translate (3M CWS users, 4.0 stars, with a dedicated Hulu page and LLM engines) are the strongest modern rivals. The only tool that names Hulu among both the large players and the AI-based ones is Immersive Translate. The dedicated Hulu dual-subtitle extensions are tiny (541 to 724 users, 5 ratings each).

**Why users switch tools**
- From Language Reactor: stagnation, unfixed bugs, broken ASR. Alternatives are criticized for price ("money grabs") or limited languages (Migaku, Trancy) — [LR forum](https://forum.languagelearningwithnetflix.com/t/language-reactor-alternative/36445).
- Language Reactor's 4.2 stars versus Trancy's 4.7 and eJOY's 4.8 (snippet) is consistent with this, but review texts were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Hulu is underserved.** Language Reactor, Trancy, Dualsub, and InterSub do not list Hulu. Immersive Translate is the only large, LLM-capable competitor with explicit Hulu support, and it is a general-purpose translator rather than a subtitle-focused product. The two dedicated Hulu extensions have fewer than 1K users each and appear stale.
- Users show they will leave over a lack of maintenance (the Language Reactor case), so visible, frequent updates are a competitive lever.
- Users push back on price for AI-heavy tools (FluentAI, Lingopie). Transparent pricing or bring-your-own-key options could differentiate.

### Gaps
- Language Reactor's official pricing was not verified because its site returned no content. The figures come from a competitor blog.
- Trancy's "1M+ downloads / 800K registered users" and Immersive Translate's "30M+" are self-reported, and they conflict with the CWS counts (300K and 3M).
- eJOY figures come from a search snippet only. I found no data for Mate's user counts, SubText's pricing and users, or Safari and mobile store ratings.
- I found no Product Hunt or YouTube comparison data in this pass.
